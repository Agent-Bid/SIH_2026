# Adaptive Sonar Engine — ESP32-S3 + FreeRTOS (100–500 kHz)

This is the final firmware package for the "ESP32 decides, FPGA transmits" design.
This file explains **what the code does in simple words**, **every assumption and correction I made**, and **what the team still has to decide**.

---

## 1. The idea in 60 seconds

The sonar sits in water. The water changes (temperature, salinity, depth, cloudiness) and the battery drains.
Sound at 500 kHz gives very sharp detail but dies quickly in water; sound at 100 kHz travels far but is less sharp.

So the ESP32 does one job for every ping, 6 times a second:

1. **Read 7 inputs**: temperature, salinity, depth, turbidity, battery, target range and the
   target's relative speed. For now they come from a built-in simulation of changing water
   conditions (`water_sim.h`, below); they can also be typed on the serial console or read
   from potentiometers.
2. **Work out the water**: how fast sound travels, how much of it is absorbed at each frequency, how much the murky water and distance weaken it, how noisy the sea is.
3. **Choose a waveform "profile"**: the finest detail that still works with enough signal margin, **unless that costs much more energy** than the cheapest profile that works (the detail-vs-energy rule, section 4).
4. **Set the power just high enough** (so it doesn't waste battery).
5. **Choose the modulation from the motion**: motion shifts every frequency (Doppler), so over
   one pulse the phase drifts fd·T cycles (fd = speed / c × fc). BPSK (Barker-13) while the
   drift is at most 0.25 cycles (cleanest when still), LFM up to 1, geometric sweep beyond
   (tolerates the most motion). Thresholds in `sonar_config.h` section 4c.
6. **Send the choice to the FPGA over SPI.** The FPGA makes the actual pulse.

**The ML model picks the profile, its amplitude and the modulation; physics checks all three.** Three small neural networks (trained on the physics' own decisions, `ml/`) propose them. The physics keeps the profile when it closes the link within the energy allowance (otherwise it chooses itself), raises the amplitude if it is too low, and replaces a modulation that cannot take the Doppler drift. With `USE_ML_MODEL 0` the physics alone decides.

**Simulated water conditions** (`water_sim.h`, the default at power-on; `A` on the serial
console returns to it): a vehicle dives and climbs between 20 and 350 m through a thermocline
(22 → 5 °C) and a halocline, turbidity drifts (plumes, and a muddy layer near the bottom), the
battery drains (and recharges at 15 %), and the sonar tracks three contacts, one per ping in
turn: A near (15–180 m) and lively, B mid-range (250–600 m) and nearly still, C far (450–1000
m) and moving. Ranges and speeds follow smooth random motions, every reading has a little
noise, and mission time runs 10× faster than real time. It is deterministic from its seed.

## 3. The 12 profiles

A profile = one ready-made chirp: centre frequency, sweep width (bandwidth), pulse length.
They are ordered **best detail first**; the code takes the first one that works.
(All are linear-FM chirps. Each keeps its full band inside 100–500 kHz.)

| ID | Name | Band | Centre | Bandwidth | Pulse | Range resolution* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 / 1 / 2 | X-S / X-M / X-L | 380–500 kHz | 440 kHz | 120 kHz | 1 / 5 / 20 ms | ~0.6 cm |
| 3 / 4 / 5 | H-S / H-M / H-L | 270–330 kHz | 300 kHz | 60 kHz | 1 / 5 / 20 ms | ~1.3 cm |
| 6 / 7 / 8 | M-S / M-M / M-L | 180–220 kHz | 200 kHz | 40 kHz | 1 / 5 / 20 ms | ~1.9 cm |
| 9 / 10 / 11 | L-S / L-M / L-L | 100–140 kHz | 120 kHz | 40 kHz | 1 / 5 / 20 ms | ~1.9 cm |

\*resolution = c / (2 × bandwidth). ID 11 (L-L) is the "best effort" fallback: lowest frequency, longest pulse.

**Why these letters?** Frequency tier (X, H, M, L) controls detail vs reach. Pulse length (S, M, L) controls how much energy goes out per ping.

### The one physics fact that shapes everything
After pulse compression, the signal-to-noise ratio depends on **pulse energy** (power × time), **not on bandwidth**.
So:
- **Bandwidth** buys sharper resolution and costs nothing in SNR.
- **Longer pulse or more power** buys SNR.
- **Higher frequency** buys detail but loses signal faster (absorption grows steeply).

That's why within a frequency tier the code prefers the **shortest** pulse that fits (fast updates, small blind zone) and only goes to longer pulses when the peak-power limit forces it.

A second fact follows from the same physics: **a lower frequency always needs less energy** (water absorbs less, thermal noise is lower). With the detail-vs-energy rule, the longer pulses of the X, H and M tiers are therefore never chosen: they are only needed when the short pulse would exceed the 20 W peak power, which happens at long range, where a lower tier is always much cheaper. In practice the choice is among X-S, H-S, M-S, L-S, L-M and L-L.

## 4. How the decision works, step by step

For each profile, best detail first:

1. **Sound speed** from Mackenzie (temperature, salinity, depth).
2. **Absorption** at the profile's centre frequency from Francois-Garrison (temperature, salinity, depth, pH).
3. **Turbidity loss** (extra attenuation from suspended particles).
4. **Spreading loss** = 20·log10(range).
5. **Total transmission loss** = spreading + (absorption + turbidity) × distance.
6. **Noise** = thermal noise + wind noise + receiver noise figure.
7. **Power needed** so that output SNR ≥ 12 dB threshold + 3 dB margin.
8. **Check limits**: needed power ≤ transmitter maximum, and needed energy ≤ what the battery allows per ping, and the chirp fits in the band.
9. **Energy per ping** of every profile that fits: power × pulse length (just enough power, never below 5 % amplitude).
10. **Energy allowance** = the larger of `DETAIL_ENERGY_FACTOR` (4) × the cheapest profile's energy, and `DETAIL_FREE_ENERGY_FRAC` (1 %) × this ping's energy budget. Extra detail is worth up to 4× the minimum energy; energy below 1 % of the budget is too small to be worth saving. The budget shrinks with the battery, so a low battery leans towards saving energy.
11. **Take the finest profile that fits within the allowance.**
12. If nothing fits → last profile at maximum allowed power, flagged `BEST_EFFORT`.

**Anti flip-flop:** to move *up* to a finer profile than the current one, the link needs 2 dB extra margin (which also raises the energy it is charged with). Moving down happens immediately. The same check applies to the ML's pick, so a jittery sensor at a profile boundary cannot make the waveform flip back and forth.

**Tuning:** `DETAIL_ENERGY_FACTOR 1` always takes the cheapest profile (nearly always L-S: the worst detail); a very large value takes the finest profile that works (the older rule).

## 5. RTOS structure (why the CPU won't stall)

Four tasks, connected by "latest value" queues (length 1, overwrite; plus a short log queue for the PING lines). Nobody waits on anybody, and a slow task can never build a backlog.

```
 core 1                                             core 0
 ┌─────────────┐  latest sample  ┌───────────────┐  latest packet  ┌────────────┐
 │ sensorTask  │ ──────────────▶ │ decisionTask  │ ──────────────▶ │  spiTask   │──▶ FPGA
 │ 50 Hz, prio2│                 │ 6 Hz, prio 3  │                 │50 Hz, prio4│
 │ ADC+filter  │                 │ sim + ML +    │                 └────────────┘
 └─────────────┘                 │ physics       │                 ┌─────────────┐
                                 └──────┬────────┘   telemetry     │ consoleTask │ prio 1
                                        └────────────────────────▶ │ Serial+cmds │
                                                                   └─────────────┘
```

- **sensorTask**: reads 5 pots (16-sample average each) and smooths them, or passes on the typed inputs; publishes the latest. (Not used in automatic mode.)
- **decisionTask**: one decision per ping, 6 a second (exactly on average). In automatic mode it steps the simulation first. Runs the networks and the physics; keeps the anti flip-flop memory per contact. It never touches SPI or Serial, so it can't be blocked by them.
- **spiTask**: highest priority, every 20 ms, sends the latest packet, so a new decision reaches the FPGA well before its next ping.
- **consoleTask**: lowest priority. Serial commands, the PING lines, the reports.
- If sensor data is older than 500 ms, decisions are skipped and the last good packet keeps going.
- The Arduino `loop()` does nothing; all work is in tasks.

**Serial console** (115200; open it with DTR and RTS off, e.g. `host/esp_console.py` in the FPGA project):
- `A`: automatic, simulated conditions (the default). One line per ping:
  `PING seq=… contact=… T=… S=… D=… turb=… bat=… R=… v=… profile=… mod=… amp=… ml_profile=… ml_mod=… ml_amp=… flags=… doppler=…`
- `W`: type the seven inputs one by one (Enter keeps a value, `q` cancels), then a full report: what each network picked and what the physics did with it, the result, and the values sent to the FPGA. `I T S D turb bat R v`: the same on one line, ending with a `DECISION` line for programs.
- `R 450`: target range. `M lfm | geo | bpsk | cw | auto`: force a modulation (`auto` = from the motion). `P`: potentiometers. `L`: status line. `?`: help.
- `C [amp]`: DAC view. Sends the FPGA a slow-motion copy of the current ping (frequencies ÷ up to 100, so the ADC can follow), records the DAC filter's output on GPIO 1 with the ADC (up to 80 kS/s), and prints it (`CAP` header, `D` lines of hex millivolts, `END`) for `host/dac_view.py` in the FPGA project. `S [n] [r]`: the same for the next n decisions (default 6), each recorded r times (default 4), with a `COND` line of conditions before each; for `host/dac_sequence.py`.
- The console writes in small pieces and waits for each to leave: the USB-Serial/JTAG driver loses text that is queued faster than the host reads it, and `Serial.flush()` can discard it.

## 6. Packet to the FPGA (57 bytes, little-endian, SPI mode 0)

Version 3. The MCU sends every waveform number **in the FPGA's own units**, so the FPGA never
divides. FTW ("frequency tuning word") = f × 2³² / F_CLK, with F_CLK = 27 MHz × 13/7 =
50.142857 MHz (`FPGA_CLK_HZ` in `sonar_config.h`). The FPGA reads byte 5 and bytes 28–55.

| Bytes | Field | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `sync` | always `0xA5` |
| 1 | `version` | 3 |
| 2–3 | `seq` | counter, increments on each new decision |
| 4 | `profileId` | 0–11 |
| 5 | `modulation` | **FPGA:** 0 = CW, 1 = LFM chirp, 2 = geometric sweep (both up, fc−bw/2 → fc+bw/2), 3 = BPSK Barker-13 at fc |
| 6 | `spreadingFactor` | round(log2(bw × T)) for the sweeps |
| 7 | `flags` | bit0 link OK, bit1 low battery, bit2 input clamped, bit3 ML used, bit4 best effort, bit5 ML overridden, bit6 ML amplitude raised |
| 8–11 | `centerFreqHz` | informational |
| 12–15 | `bandwidthHz` | informational |
| 16–19 | `pulseUs` | informational: pulse duration in µs |
| 20–21 | `amplitude` | informational: 0–65535 = 0–100 % drive |
| 22–23 | `txPowerMilliW` | electrical, informational |
| 24–27 | `propDelayUs` | one-way delay to target range |
| 28–31 | `lenClk` | **FPGA:** pulse length in FPGA clocks (max 2²⁴−1 ≈ 335 ms; BPSK: 13 whole chips) |
| 32–35 | `ftwStart` | **FPGA:** start frequency as an FTW (fc for CW/BPSK, fc − bw/2 for the sweeps) |
| 36–39 | `ftwStep` | **FPGA:** LFM: FTW change per clock × 2¹⁶ (signed). Geometric: (ratio − 1) × 2³², applied every 32 clocks, ratio = (f_end/f_start)^(1/(lenClk/32)). CW/BPSK: 0 |
| 40–43 | `winStep` | **FPGA:** 2³² / lenClk (the Hann window makes one lap per pulse) |
| 44–45 | `ampQ12` | **FPGA:** amplitude, 4096 = 1.0 |
| 46–47 | `code` | **FPGA:** BPSK phase code, chip i = bit i, 1 = flip (Barker-13 = `0x0A60`); else 0 |
| 48–51 | `chipLen` | **FPGA:** BPSK clocks per chip; else 0 |
| 52–55 | `periodClk` | **FPGA:** clocks from one pulse start to the next: `PING_PERIOD_MIN_S` (0.1 s), or pulse + round trip in echo mode |
| 56 | `crc8` | CRC-8 (poly 0x07, init 0) over bytes 0–55 |

The 16 fractional bits of the LFM step matter: a 20 ms, 40 kHz chirp needs a step of 3.416 FTW
per clock, and a whole-number step would sweep 12 % too little. `validateProfiles()` checks at
boot that every profile fits these fields with every modulation.

The FPGA owner must confirm the SPI mode, clock, byte order and whether they want CRC-checked packets (see section 8).

### 7a. Corrections (things in earlier versions that would not work, now fixed)

| # | What was wrong | What it is now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Thorp absorption** was in the earlier code and the team doc | **Francois-Garrison (1982)** | Thorp is a low-frequency fit. Francois-Garrison is published for 200 Hz–1 MHz, so it covers 100–500 kHz, and it uses temperature, salinity, depth and pH, which your doc says absorption should depend on. Thorp ignores temperature, salinity and depth. Example (35 ppt, surface): at 300 kHz Thorp says 67 dB/km, Francois-Garrison says 66 dB/km at 5 °C but 137 dB/km at 30 °C; at 500 kHz Thorp says 112 dB/km, Francois-Garrison says 129 (5 °C) to 199 (30 °C). ## 8. What will NOT work until the team decides (blockers)

1. **Transducer coverage.** One transducer rarely covers a 5:1 frequency span (100–500 kHz) with 40–120 kHz sweeps. The firmware assumes it can. If the hardware has separate transducers per band, the profile table and the FPGA switching logic must reflect that. Ask the hardware owner first.
2. **FPGA/DAC speed.** To synthesize up to 500 kHz the DAC/FPGA path must run well above 1 MS/s (Nyquist); realistically 2.5 MS/s or more. Confirm with the FPGA owner.
3. **SPI contract.** Agreed with the FPGA side: SPI mode 0, 2 MHz, packet v3 above, CRC-checked; no ready/ack line. **Verified on the boards (2026-09-30)**: 10 packets/s, none rejected, and the FPGA plays the chosen waveform bit-exact. The FPGA frames packets by the pause after each burst, because the CS wire (GPIO14 → pin 36) does not carry a clean signal on the current boards; the firmware still drives CS as usual.
4. **One-way or echo sonar?** This changes the loss maths (`ACTIVE_ECHO_MODE`). The ML dataset assumes one-way.
5. **Selection policy.** Decided: the detail-vs-energy rule (section 4). Still to tune: the two numbers in `sonar_config.h` section 4b (then retrain the ML).
6. **Turbidity attenuation** needs a real calibration (a turbidity sensor + a known-range acoustic test).
7. **Where the range comes from.** For now it is typed over Serial. If the mission needs it automatic, someone must supply an estimate.

## 10. The ML model

- **What runs** (`adaptive_sonar/ml_model.h`, 30 KB of flash, called by `mlSelect()` and `mlModulation()`):
  - **profile network** 6 → 48 → 48 → 6 (ReLU): one score for each profile the rule ever picks
    (X-S, H-S, M-S, L-S, L-M, L-L); the highest wins.
  - **amplitude network** 12 → 48 → 48 → 1: the 6 inputs plus the chosen profile (one-hot) →
    log(amplitude that just closes the link); a small safety margin (+2.3 %) is added so that it
    is rarely short. The firmware applies the 5 % floor and the power limits.
  - **modulation network** 16 → 32 → 32 → 3: temperature, salinity, depth, log10(speed + 0.01)
    and the profile the physics settled on (one-hot of 12) → LFM, geometric or BPSK.
- **Roles**: the ML always answers. `decide()` keeps its profile when it closes the link within
  the energy allowance, with the anti flip-flop margin (`FLAG_ML_USED`), and otherwise chooses
  itself (`FLAG_ML_OVERRIDDEN`). It uses the ML's amplitude for the ML's profile, raising it to
  what the link needs if it is too low (`FLAG_ML_AMP_RAISED`). `chooseModulation()` keeps the
  ML's modulation when it can take the Doppler drift, else uses the rule's
  (`FLAG_ML_MOD_OVERRIDDEN`).
- **Inputs (6)**: temperature, salinity, depth, turbidity, battery, **range** (as log10).
- **Data**: 300,000 scenarios labelled by `tools/physics_reference.py` (the same
  `sonar_config.h` as the firmware), half with range drawn log-uniformly; tested on
  2 × 20,000 scenarios with other seeds.
- **Results** (`ml/REPORT.md`): the profile agrees with the physics on 99.2 % (uniform range) /
  99.4 % (log range) of scenarios, and 0.5 % of picks are overridden. XGBoost reached 97–99 %
  with ~20,000 tree nodes, a decision tree 94–96 %. The amplitude is within 1.8 % (median) of
  what the link needs; it costs on average 1.6–3.4 % more energy than the minimum, and is raised
  by the physics in 1–2 % of cases. The modulation agrees with the rule on 99.7 % of 20,000
  test cases (0.2 % overridden); every disagreement is within ~1 % of a threshold.
- **Why it is not better than physics**: its labels come from the physics, so it can only copy
  it. It improves on the physics only once it is retrained on measured results (for example the
  received level per ping).
- **Retrain** after any change to `sonar_config.h` that affects the choice (profiles, power,
  thresholds, the detail-vs-energy knobs, sensor ranges):
  `python3 -m venv ml/.venv && ml/.venv/bin/pip install -r ml/requirements.txt && ml/.venv/bin/python ml/train.py`
  (about 5 minutes on a laptop CPU), then run `bash tools/run_tests.sh`.

## 11. Wiring and board settings

- Pots (wiper → pin, ends → 3.3 V and GND): temperature GPIO1, salinity GPIO2, depth GPIO3, turbidity GPIO4, battery GPIO5 (all ADC1).
- FPGA SPI: CS GPIO14 → Tang Nano pin 36, MOSI GPIO12 → pin 37, SCK GPIO13 → pin 38, MISO GPIO11 → pin 39 (unused by both sides), plus a GND wire. The Tang Nano pins are in its 3.3 V bank. SPI mode 0, 2 MHz, MSB first, CS low for the whole 57-byte packet.
- Avoid GPIO 26–37 (flash/PSRAM on N16R8), 19/20 (USB), 0/3/45/46 (strapping).
- Arduino IDE: board *ESP32S3 Dev Module*, Flash 16 MB, PSRAM *Disabled* (unused), *USB CDC On Boot: Enabled* if using the native USB port. Serial 115200.
